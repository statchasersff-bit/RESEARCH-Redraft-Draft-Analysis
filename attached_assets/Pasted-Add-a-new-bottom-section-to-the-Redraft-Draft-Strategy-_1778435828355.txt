Title:
NFL Rule Changes

Subtitle:
Why RB-heavy strategies may have rebounded after 2023

Body copy:
Across the 2021–2025 sample, most redraft strategy archetypes did not show a clean year-over-year trend. WR-heavy, Late TE, and Balanced builds were relatively stable, while several other strategies moved around without forming an obvious long-term pattern.

The biggest exception was RB-heavy construction. Robust RB struggled from 2021–2023, then jumped sharply in 2024 and remained strong in 2025. Zero RB showed the opposite pattern, performing well earlier in the sample before falling off in 2024 and 2025.

One possible explanation is the changing NFL rule environment. In 2023, the league expanded Impermissible Use of the Helmet rules, making it a foul for players to use the helmet to butt, ram, spear, or make forcible contact to an opponent’s head or neck area. In 2024, the NFL eliminated the swivel hip-drop tackle, penalizing defenders who wrap the runner, unweight themselves, swivel/drop the hips, and land on or trap the runner’s legs.

Because running backs absorb high collision volume and are especially exposed to lower-body injuries, these rule changes may have contributed to better RB availability and more stable RB production. That could help explain why RB-first strategies became more successful in 2024–2025 compared to 2021–2023.

Important note:
This does not prove causation. The shift could also be influenced by ADP changes, player pool strength, offensive trends, injuries, scoring environment, or market overcorrections. But the timing is notable enough that it should be included as context when interpreting the strategy data.

Add a final takeaway callout:
The fantasy environment may be shifting back toward RB-first constructions after several years where WR-heavy and Zero RB approaches carried more appeal.
